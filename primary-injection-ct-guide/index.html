How it works

Magnetic field: Biot–Savart

B(r) = μ₀ I / (4π) ∮ [dℓ × (r − r′)] / |r − r′|³

The field is the vector sum from a closed, piecewise-straight loop. Each straight segment uses its analytic finite-wire solution. Rounded cable bends are approximated by short segments. The internal return path is an explicit assumption, not a transformer winding model.

Arrows follow the calculated vector. Streamlines are numerically integrated through that same field, not drawn as predetermined circles. Arrow length is logarithmically scaled; streamline density is illustrative. This is a filament-current approximation outside the conductor radius. The model excludes conductor interiors, skin/proximity current redistribution, magnetic materials, and displacement current.

Ampère: enclosure is what matters

∮ H · dℓ = I_enclosed; in modeled air, H = B / μ₀

The field solver checks its closed contour against the enclosed current. A perfect CT does not become inaccurate simply because the primary is off-center. Real winding asymmetry, core/joint properties, external fields, excitation and electronics can create position dependence. Enclosing outgoing and return currents together gives their algebraic sum, not the current in one branch.

AC phase and RMS

i(t) = √2 I_rms sin(2πft); B(t) = √2 B_rms sin(2πft)

The visualized direction reverses each half-cycle. Steady-state RMS readings do not drop to zero at an instantaneous zero crossing. The added uniform field is assumed in phase; no DC offset, harmonics, switching transient, or remanence is simulated.

Burden is not saturation

N = N_s/N_p; I₀ = I_p/N
Z_t = R_w + R_leads + R_b + jX_b
Y_e = 1/R_c + 1/(j2πfL_m)
I_s = I₀ / (1 + Z_tY_e)

Burden is the secondary load, including meter and leads. Winding resistance adds internal voltage demand. The example circuit splits the referred primary current between the load and excitation branches. L_m and R_c are constant assumed parameters, so this is only a linear steady-state example.

E_required ≈ |I₀ Z_t|
B_peak,required ≈ E_required / (4.4429 f N_s A_c)
V_k(f) ≈ V_k,ref × f/f_ref

Faraday’s law relates secondary voltage demand to core flux. Comparing the ideal-transfer voltage demand with an entered knee voltage is a screening calculation, not an exact saturation threshold or accuracy guarantee. At or above that knee, the app withholds the linear CT reading. Actual saturation needs the excitation curve, core losses, waveform and remanence.

Shunt example

R₀ = V_rated/I_rated
R(T) = R₀[1 + α ΔT × 10⁻⁶]
V_rms = I_rms √[R(T)² + (2πfL)²]
I_indicated = V_rms/R₀; P = I_rms² R(T)

This readout assumes a meter divides measured RMS voltage by nominal resistance without temperature or inductance correction. A real calibrated AC measurement chain may correct these effects.

Variable definitions

I_p, I_s, I₀: primary, actual secondary and ideal referred secondary current (A RMS). B: magnetic flux density (T); 1 mT = 1000 μT. H: magnetic field strength (A/m). μ₀: free-space permeability, approximated as 4π × 10⁻⁷ H/m.

N_p, N_s: primary and secondary turns; this circuit assumes N_p = 1. f: frequency (Hz). j: imaginary unit for phasors. Φ: flux (Wb), with induced voltage e = −N dΦ/dt.

R_b, X_b: burden resistance and reactance (Ω). R_leads: complete secondary lead-loop resistance (Ω). R_w: winding resistance at operating temperature (Ω). L_m: secondary-referred magnetizing inductance (H). R_c: secondary-referred core-loss resistance (Ω).

A_c: effective magnetic core cross-section (cm² in the input, converted to m²). V_k,ref: entered knee voltage at its reference frequency. α: shunt temperature coefficient (ppm/°C). ΔT: change from shunt calibration temperature (°C). L: lumped residual shunt inductance (nH in the input).

What this cannot determine

No percentage is assigned to steel walls, transformer leakage, a jaw gap, or “core nonlinearity.” Lowering an arbitrary slider would not make those effects more accurate. Steel can alter a field through its geometry, permeability, saturation and eddy currents; transformer leakage requires actual source geometry or measurements. Free-space B at the CT location is not the field inside its working magnetic core.

The example cabinet is visual context only. Midspan is a repeatable starting location, not a proven optimum. Use the monitoring CT’s installation instructions, range, bandwidth and output-loading requirements. Establish position dependence by repeatable comparison with an appropriate calibrated reference, without choosing a position merely because it happens to agree once.

Practical comparison procedure
  1. Identify sensor type, ratio or V/A sensitivity, rated current, bandwidth, required load, calibration status, and the reference instrument’s uncertainty. Confirm secondary closure and burden with the circuit de-energized.
  2. Start on a straight accessible run, use the specified aperture position and fully close any split core. Keep the arrangement repeatable and away from nearby high-current paths where practical. For parallel output leads, include all same-direction leads needed to measure the total, and exclude the return.
  3. Record simultaneous CT and reference readings over the same RMS/time window. Stop injection before repositioning. Repeat at controlled current and thermal conditions, then return to the first position to check drift.
  4. Compare repeatability and changes against the combined measurement uncertainty. Do not identify steel or transformer leakage as the cause from position sensitivity alone.
